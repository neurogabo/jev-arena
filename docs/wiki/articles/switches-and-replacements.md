# Switching, pivots, and replacements

Voluntary switches, pivots, forced replacements, entry effects, resets, and eligible reserves.

## Reference text

A voluntary switch withdraws one active Pokémon and puts an eligible reserve in its position. The replacement can face attacks aimed at that position later in the turn. A switch is not an attack by the incoming Pokémon.

Ordinary switching clears stat stages and most volatile effects. It preserves current HP, PP, and major status unless a specific effect modifies them. Field weather, terrain, side conditions, and hazards remain unless another effect removes them.

Entry hazards and entry abilities can affect the incoming Pokémon before later actions occur. A Pokémon can faint on entry. The ordering of individual entry effects matters; do not assume every entry ability activates before every hazard or that all entry effects are simultaneous.

Pivot moves such as U-turn or Volt Switch attack first and can then request a replacement if their conditions succeed. Missing, protection, an immunity, the user fainting, or a competing switch-triggered effect can prevent the expected pivot. A damaging pivot's target changing or fainting is not the same as the user voluntarily switching before the attack.

Forced switches such as Roar and Dragon Tail have their own failure conditions and generally choose a reserve through the engine rather than letting the affected player freely choose. Baton Pass explicitly transfers certain effects that an ordinary switch clears.

A reserve must actually be available in the current battle. Team-preview Pokémon are not automatically all selected reserves. Both active Pokémon cannot switch into the same reserve simultaneously.
