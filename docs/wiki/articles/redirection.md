# Redirection

Follow Me, Rage Powder, and ability-based redirection: valid targets, timing, powder immunity, and exceptions.

## Reference text

Include when Follow Me, Rage Powder, Lightning Rod, Storm Drain, or a redirection-bypassing effect is relevant. Dependencies: target eligibility, action order, powder immunity, ability suppression.

Follow Me and Rage Powder normally act at priority +2 and establish redirection for the remainder of the turn. Suitable opposing single-target moves are redirected to the user while it is a valid target. They do not generally redirect an entire spread move, a self-targeted move, or a field effect.

Redirection begins only after the redirecting effect is established. Fake Out at +3 can act before a normally ordered Follow Me or Rage Powder. A redirector that faints or becomes an invalid target cannot simply continue drawing every later attack.

Rage Powder additionally depends on whether the attacker is affected by powder. A Grass-type attacker or one protected by an applicable powder-immunity ability can ignore Rage Powder. Follow Me does not share this powder limitation.

Lightning Rod draws eligible single-target Electric moves to its holder and Storm Drain does the same for Water moves. They can interact with moves from either side; deliberately targeting an ally can therefore matter. Their type immunity and Special Attack increase are separate from whether redirection was needed.

Follow Me/Rage Powder have precedence over those ability-based redirection effects when their conditions are met. Competing ability redirectors use their specific ordering rules; the fastest applicable holder normally wins, with ability activation order relevant to the documented tie case. Snipe Shot and other explicit bypass effects need their own exception entries.
