# Special damage and multi-hit attacks

Fixed and reactive damage, OHKO rules, variable hit counts, between-hit triggers, and self-damage families.

## Reference text

Include the relevant family when a considered move uses it. Dependencies: exact move definition, immunity, Substitute, contact/secondary events, HP history.

Fixed-damage moves and HP-setting effects do not use the ordinary power/offense/defense formula. Seismic Toss-style level damage, Super Fang's current-HP fraction, Final Gambit's use of the attacker's current HP, and Pain Split's HP averaging are different mechanisms. Their own type immunities, failure checks, and Substitute interactions still matter.

Counter-like attacks depend on a qualifying earlier hit in the same turn. Counter uses qualifying physical damage, Mirror Coat uses qualifying special damage, and Metal Burst has its own broader rule and multiplier. The target may be the last qualifying attacker rather than the originally preferred foe. Only the relevant last hit, not necessarily the entire total of a multi-hit attack, supplies their damage history.

One-hit knockout moves use special accuracy, level, and immunity rules. They are not guaranteed knockouts merely because their effect is called “one-hit KO,” and increasing ordinary offensive stats does not turn them into ordinary high-power attacks. Sturdy and move-specific type immunities can stop them.

For ordinary attacks with a variable two-to-five hit count, the hit-count distribution is 35% for two, 35% for three, 15% for four, and 15% for five, before modifying effects. Skill Link can change this. Other multi-hit moves can have a fixed count or individual accuracy checks.

Each hit can interact with Substitute, full-HP protection, contact reactions, berries, and current HP. Exact HP-threshold timing for Anger Shell and Berserk during multi-hit attacks is uncertain.

Recoil, crash damage, self-KO, and HP costs are separate categories. A missed ordinary recoil move does not necessarily behave like a missed jump-kick-style crash move. An Explosion-style self-KO does not require the same conditions as Final Gambit. Use the actual move's rule rather than a generic “self damage” label.
