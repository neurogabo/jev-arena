# Action order

Priority brackets, effective Speed, ties, dynamic ordering, switches, and Mega Evolution timing.

## Reference text

For ordinary actions in this environment, voluntary switches execute before Mega Evolution, and Mega Evolution executes before ordinary moves. Entry effects caused by those switches can change the field before the attacks occur. Explicit preparation effects and interrupting events have their own timing.

Move priority is considered before Speed. A higher priority bracket acts before a lower one regardless of the ordinary Speed comparison. Within the same priority bracket, faster Pokémon normally act first. Equal effective Speed can be resolved randomly. Specific effects can force a Pokémon earlier or later within an order bracket.

Some illustrative move priorities are:

| Move or family | Ordinary priority |
| --- | ---: |
| Helping Hand | +5 |
| Protect and Detect | +4 |
| Fake Out, Wide Guard, Quick Guard | +3 |
| Follow Me, Rage Powder, Feint, First Impression | +2 |
| Sucker Punch | +1 |
| Most attacks and many status moves | 0 |
| Roar and Dragon Tail | -6 |
| Trick Room | -7 |

These values do not replace the current definition of a move. Abilities or other effects can alter priority. For example, Prankster raises the priority of status moves. An effect that makes a Pokémon act early within its bracket does not necessarily give its move positive priority for Psychic Terrain or Quick Guard.

Speed is updated as the turn progresses. A Speed change, paralysis, Tailwind, weather-dependent Speed ability, Mega Evolution, or other applicable change can reorder actions that have not executed. A Pokémon that has already acted does not receive another ordinary action merely because Speed changes.

Trick Room reverses the Speed comparison within a priority bracket. It does not reverse priority brackets or make an ordinary negative-priority move precede a positive-priority move. Trick Room has no extreme-Speed wraparound in Champions.
