# Yawn, attraction, and Perish Song

Delayed sleep, source-linked attraction, Perish Song counters, switching, and relevant prevention.

## Reference text

Include only the subrules relevant to considered effects. Dependencies: status prevention, volatile effects, switching, residual order, sound immunity for Perish Song.

Yawn creates a delayed sleep effect. It normally attempts to put the affected Pokémon to sleep at the end of the following turn if that Pokémon is still active and can then fall asleep. Ordinary switching clears the pending Yawn effect. Existing major status and sleep-prevention effects can prevent initial application or later sleep. Electric Terrain blocks Yawn for grounded Pokémon; Misty Terrain can allow the drowsy effect but prevents grounded Pokémon from actually falling asleep from it. Safeguard's initial-application and already-pending-Yawn behavior are different; see M24.

Attraction is a volatile source-linked effect, separate from major status. When it can be established under the move's gender and immunity conditions, a reached attraction check has a 50% chance to prevent the affected Pokémon's move. A genderless or same-gender pairing does not satisfy ordinary Attract's requirement. Leaving the field, losing the relevant source, or an explicit cure can end it. Include the precise source and prevention rule when this effect is used.

Perish Song assigns a perish counter to affected active Pokémon that do not already have one. The counter decreases at the end of the use turn and each subsequent turn: the displayed sequence proceeds through 3, 2, 1, and then 0, at which point the affected Pokémon faints. Ordinary switching removes the count; Baton Pass can transfer it. Protect and ordinary damage resistance do not turn Perish Song into an ordinary blocked attack. Soundproof can prevent the sound-based application. A Pokémon's existing perish counter is not simply refreshed by another use.
