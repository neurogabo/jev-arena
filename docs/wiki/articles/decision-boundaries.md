# Decision and information boundaries

Winning objective, legal joint choices, disclosed information, and the distinction between observations and hypotheses.

## Reference text

Select a supplied legal joint action for the current request. In an ordinary doubles move request, a joint action specifies what both active Pokémon will do, including any selected targets, switches, and permitted Mega Evolution declaration. Evaluate how the two actions work together.

The objective is to improve the chance of winning the whole battle. Immediate damage is one consideration alongside survival, removing threats, protecting a partner, controlling action order, preserving useful reserves, creating favorable positions, and managing limited resources. These are decision objectives, not additional battle mechanics.

Treat the supplied current observation as the factual description of this decision point. Distinguish confirmed information, explicitly supplied open-set information, estimates, and hypotheses. A common opponent set is not a confirmed set. A possible opponent response is not its announced action. An unknown item is different from a confirmed absence of an item.

Use the supplied Champions-specific definitions. Do not fill a gap by silently borrowing a value from another Pokémon game. A species can have a different form, current type, ability, or effective statistics from its usual starting form. Current battle transformations and revealed effects matter.

Only supplied candidates can be selected. A candidate's inclusion means it can be submitted under the information available when the list was assembled; it does not guarantee that the move will hit, that its effect will succeed, or that no newly revealed restriction will invalidate it. An attack into an immunity can still be a selectable action.
