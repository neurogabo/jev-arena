# Confusion and flinching

Confusion counters and self-hits; flinch timing, prevention, and the distinction from attack damage.

## Reference text

Include when either effect exists or can be created. Dependencies: action order, volatile effects, Substitute and relevant prevention abilities.

Confusion is a volatile effect and can coexist with major status. Ordinary confusion initializes its counter uniformly to 2, 3, 4, or 5. Each reached confusion check first decrements that counter; reaching zero ends confusion before any self-hit roll. Consequently, without an intervening cure or modifier, there are one through four checks at which self-hitting is possible, followed by the check that ends confusion. Some sources, such as Axe Kick, have a different minimum initial counter.

At a reached check while still confused, the self-hit probability is **33/100**. A self-hit prevents the selected move. It uses the Pokémon's own Attack and Defense, including their stages, with a 40-power physical-like calculation and ordinary damage variation. This calculation does not run the ordinary damage-modifier chain: it has no ordinary type matchup, STAB, critical hit, screen reduction, or burn damage penalty. Ordinary switching removes confusion unless an explicit transfer effect carries it.

Flinching prevents the affected Pokémon's move when it reaches the flinch check in that turn. It does not remove an action already completed and does not normally carry into the next turn. An attacker moving after its target cannot retroactively cancel that target's completed move through flinching.

Inner Focus prevents flinching. Shield Dust can prevent a flinch that is a blocked secondary effect. Preventing the flinch does not necessarily prevent the attack's damage. Fake Out's damage and its flinch should therefore be considered separately.
