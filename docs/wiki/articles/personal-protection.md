# Protect, Detect, and related personal protection

Protect-family scope, Champions PP, diminishing success, contact retaliation, and protection bypass versus removal.

## Reference text

Include when a relevant protection move is known or hypothesized. Dependencies: priority, move blockability, consecutive-use counter, bypass effects, residual damage.

Protect and Detect normally create personal protection for the remainder of the current turn, with priority +4. They block eligible moves from other Pokémon, including many damaging and status moves. They do not protect the partner automatically and do not stop already established residual effects such as burn, Leech Seed, or Salt Cure.

Success depends on the shared consecutive-protection counter. Starting fresh, the normal success probability is 1. After a success, the next relevant attempt is 1/3, then 1/9, 1/27, and so on, with the pinned counter capped at 729. A failure or a sufficient break in the chain resets the effect. Related protection moves can contribute to the same counter; switching from Protect to Detect does not provide a fresh guaranteed attempt. A protection move can also fail if no eligible action remains for it to precede.

In Champions, Protect's base PP is 5. That base value is not the current or maximum PP supplied for an individual Pokémon: the Champions PP transformation gives an ordinary base-5 move 8 maximum PP. Always use the request's per-Pokémon PP.

Personal protection variants have different scopes and punishments. King's Shield blocks damaging moves and can lower a contact attacker's Attack; it is not general protection against status moves. Spiky Shield can damage a contacting attacker. Baneful Bunker can poison one. Include the exact variant's retaliation condition, amount, and immunity checks when it appears.

Feint and particular other moves can break protection so later moves benefit. Merely bypassing protection for one hit is different from removing the protective effect for the rest of the turn. Use the supplied bypass or break behavior rather than assuming every exception is equivalent.
