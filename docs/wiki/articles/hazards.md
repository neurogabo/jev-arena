# Entry hazards and hazard removal

Entry hazards, layers, grounding, typing, prevention, absorption, and distinct removal methods.

## Reference text

Include when hazards are present or a considered move can set them. Dependencies: switching, grounding, type effectiveness, status/stat-change prevention.

Hazards belong to a side and affect eligible Pokémon entering that side. Setting a hazard does not itself apply all its entry consequences to Pokémon already standing there. Ordinary switching does not remove hazards.

| Hazard | Ordinary entry effect |
| --- | --- |
| Stealth Rock | Loses 1/8 maximum HP multiplied by Rock effectiveness: commonly 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, or 1/2. It is not restricted to grounded Pokémon. |
| Spikes | Grounded entrants lose 1/8, 1/6, or 1/4 maximum HP for one, two, or three layers. |
| Toxic Spikes | Eligible grounded entrants are poisoned at one layer or badly poisoned at two. A grounded Poison-type entrant can absorb the layers. Steel immunity prevents poisoning but does not itself absorb the layers. |
| Sticky Web | Lowers the grounded entrant's Speed by one stage, subject to applicable prevention, reversal, and reaction effects. |

The currently effective ability, item, grounding state, and status immunity can change hazard consequences. A Pokémon may faint from entry damage before a later event benefits it.

Removal moves differ. Rapid Spin and Mortal Spin require their own successful interaction and remove specified effects from the user's side. Defog can remove hazards and other field/side effects under its broader rules. Tidy Up removes its specified hazards and substitutes. Do not treat these moves as interchangeable generic “clear all effects” actions.

When multiple hazards coexist, their application order can affect HP thresholds and reactions. The exact order for every combination is unspecified; do not assume a universal sequence.
