# Statuses, volatile effects, and counters

Major status, volatile conditions, side and field effects, persistence, and differing counter meanings.

## Reference text

A Pokémon ordinarily has at most one major status: burn, poison, bad poison, paralysis, sleep, or freeze. Applying another normally does not replace an existing major status. Explicit moves such as Rest have their own replacement behavior.

Confusion, flinching, attraction, Taunt, Encore, Substitute, Leech Seed, and many other temporary effects are separate from major status and can coexist with it. Side conditions and field effects are separate again.

Major status usually persists through ordinary switching; many volatile effects do not. Switching does not automatically restore HP, PP, or a consumed item. An ability such as Natural Cure or Regenerator can add a specific switch-out benefit.

Counter meanings differ. Some effects count end-of-turn phases, some count attempted actions, some count successful uses, and some count hits taken. A Pokémon merely spending a turn on the bench does not necessarily advance its sleep or freeze counter. Use the supplied counter meaning and observed history.

Known duration, elapsed duration, and uncertain remaining duration are different facts. If a duration or hidden item has not been observed, do not turn a plausible duration into an exact remaining-turn count.
